# Tacto: six-month company operating plan

**Status:** Provisional planning draft, prepared 28 September 2026. Month 1 means the first month after the founders agree to the plan; dates are not yet committed.

## 1. Scope and assumptions

Tacto's business, team, traction, funding, and geography have not yet been confirmed. The workspace contains an Android-first, device-local music-ranking prototype called MARGIN. There is no confirmed connection between that product and Tacto.

To make this draft concrete, it assumes Tacto is an early consumer-app company, potentially operating this product, with two working founders, little or no revenue, and a small initial audience. Music-specific examples apply only if that connection is confirmed. This plan needs a different acquisition, sales, staffing, and financial model if Tacto is an agency, B2B company, physical-product company, or established business.

Every numeric target below is a proposed operating target, not a forecast, industry benchmark, or statement about current performance. Rupee figures are an illustrative budget denomination, not a confirmed operating geography or researched salary quotation.

Before approving the plan, record: product and business model; target customer and market; existing staff and time commitments; current users, retention, revenue and costs; unrestricted cash; founder compensation; release readiness; and any binding deadlines. Existing staff should fill these roles before additional hiring.

## 2. Six-month objective

Establish one clearly defined audience that repeatedly receives value, a reliable product, one repeatable acquisition route, an accountable small team, and enough financial visibility to decide whether to expand, narrow the product, or change direction.

For a social music product, start with one reachable music community or genre cluster. The candidate core loop is: make a ranking, share or compare it with another person, then return for another conversation. Validate that loop rather than treating it as established demand.

By Month 6, the base planning scenario aims for approximately 3,000 cumulative eligible registrations, 1,000 monthly meaningful active users, 300 weekly meaningful active users, four-week retention of at least 20%, one acquisition route repeated successfully in two separate tests, and one measured commercial experiment. These measures do not by themselves establish product-market fit.

## 3. Company structure and accountability

Build functions with named owners before building separate departments. Four core people, plus specialist contractors, are enough for this draft's base scope.

| Function | Accountable owner | Responsibilities | Weekly evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company strategy and finance | CEO / business founder | Priorities, cash, fundraising, hiring, partnerships | Cash forecast, decisions, customer learning |
| Product and research | Product founder; CEO if no separate product founder | Audience, discovery, backlog, activation, retention | Five user conversations, funnel, one prioritized improvement |
| Engineering, reliability and security | Technical founder | Shared backend, releases, access controls, monitoring, incident response | Working release, defect list, reliability dashboard |
| Design and brand | Product owner with contract designer | Usability, identity, onboarding, reusable marketing assets | Tested flow and approved templates |
| Marketing, social and community | Growth/community generalist | Content, creator coordination, acquisition attribution, community rituals | Content results, activated users by source, response times |
| Partnerships and commercial experiments | CEO | Partner pipeline, offers, pricing tests, measured pilots | Next steps per opportunity, signed pilot or explicit rejection |
| Support and moderation | Growth/community generalist; technical founder for incidents | Support inbox, reports, escalation, community conduct | Ticket age, reported incidents, recurring friction |
| People and administration | CEO with accountant/legal specialists | Contracts, onboarding, payroll, bookkeeping, applicable obligations | Completed checklist, outstanding obligations |

The same person may own several functions, but each deliverable has exactly one accountable owner. Do not assume founder availability: record hours and reduce scope when capacity is lower than planned.

### Hiring sequence

1. **Month 1: experienced product engineer if needed.** Hire only if the technical founder cannot deliver the core production work within the agreed timeline. Assess mobile delivery, backend ownership, debugging, and communication using prior work and a paid, bounded exercise. A capable existing technical team eliminates this hire.
2. **Month 1: contract product designer.** Commission onboarding, the core flow, and share templates as a defined project. Avoid an open-ended retainer until repeated need is demonstrated.
3. **Month 2: growth/community generalist.** Combine writing, lightweight video production, community operations, and basic funnel analysis. Require examples showing qualified acquisition or community participation, not just follower counts. Hire only when there is a usable beta and an ongoing workload the founders cannot cover.
4. **Months 3–4: contract QA or editing capacity.** Buy a limited number of hours when device testing or content production is the documented bottleneck. These are services, not new departments.
5. **Months 5–6: optional support/community assistance.** Add capacity only if support exceeds ten founder/generalist hours per week for three consecutive weeks, core targets are being met, and the cash forecast supports it. Otherwise keep the core team unchanged.

Full-time hiring requires a documented workload, a 90-day role scorecard, and at least nine months of projected runway after the commitment. This is a proposed company policy. It is not a universal benchmark. Do not hire a separate CMO, sales team, HR team, or data scientist in this base plan.

New-hire onboarding: company context and access in Week 1; first bounded deliverable in Week 2; independently owned workstream by Day 30; evidence against the role scorecard at Days 60 and 90.

## 4. Monthly milestones

### Month 1 — Establish the foundation and validate the audience

**Owners:** CEO, product owner, technical founder.

- Conduct 20 interviews across candidate audiences, then select one initial segment based on actual behavior, reachable distribution, and repeated need. Include people outside the founders' friend group.
- Observe at least ten people completing the core task. Record where they stop, what they already use, and whether the result has value without prompting.
- Agree positioning: who Tacto serves, what they do with it, why they return, and how it differs from existing alternatives.
- Audit product readiness, dependencies, data handling, ownership of work, and the release path. If Tacto operates this prototype, real accounts and shared activity are substantial new work.
- Produce a narrow six-week engineering scope and event dictionary. Set up a simple company dashboard, support inbox, partner tracker, and 13-week cash forecast.
- Establish brand basics, landing page, two primary social surfaces at most, and six reusable content templates.
- Recruit roughly 50 genuinely qualified prospects and 20 hands-on testers. A qualified prospect matches the selected audience and has explicitly opted in.

**Exit decision:** Start beta work only when one segment and one core loop are selected, the priority problems are supported by interview/observation evidence, and resources are committed. If demand is weak, revise the proposition before adding scope.

### Month 2 — Deliver a usable closed beta

**Owners:** Technical founder and product owner; growth owner joins when justified.

- Ship the smallest shared experience: account access, persistent shared data, the core create/publish flow, onboarding, and basic support. For a social product, implement visibility enforcement, reporting and blocking before wider exposure.
- Make sample content clearly distinguishable from real community activity. Instrument events and verify they reflect real actions.
- Complete native-device testing for the supported platform, slow networks, older supported devices, text scaling, and account/data flows.
- Bring testers in weekly batches so the team can observe and support each group. Plan for 150 cumulative eligible registrations by month-end.
- Run one recurring community activity per week. Publish around four main pieces of social content weekly, using a manageable production batch.
- Interview five users weekly, including those who leave. Fix the three largest onboarding problems before feature expansion.

**Exit decision:** Target at least 35% activation, no unresolved critical access/data defects, and reliable completion of the core task. If fewer than 25% of 50 or more eligible new users activate, spend the next sprint correcting onboarding or the proposition rather than opening acquisition.

### Month 3 — Improve repeat use

**Owners:** Product, engineering, growth/community.

- Improve the largest measured retention problem. Candidate work includes clearer first-session prompts, useful shared content, and a reason to return; these remain hypotheses.
- Establish two weekly community prompts and a consistent response routine. Recruit five repeat contributors, not merely five people who agree to post once.
- Run two small creator/community pilots with distinct tracking links. Document incentives and commitments so subsidized activity is visible.
- Target 400 cumulative registrations, 200 monthly meaningful active users, 50 weekly meaningful active users, and activation of at least 40%.
- Measure four-week retention only for mature cohorts. Target at least 15%; report sample size and how many returning users were staff, paid contributors, or otherwise incentivized.
- Interview ten plausible buyers or partners about a specific commercial offer. Do not equate interest or a waitlist with payment.

**Exit decision:** If two mature cohorts totaling at least 100 activated users have four-week retention below 10%, pause broad acquisition and rework the core experience. Small cohorts remain directional evidence, not a reason to claim success.

### Month 4 — Launch selectively and test distribution

**Owners:** CEO, technical founder, growth owner.

- Expand to a controlled public audience only after reliability, reporting, support, visibility, and access controls are ready.
- Run two or three channel experiments sequentially: owned social, a creator collaboration, and one community partnership. Do not run more experiments than can be measured.
- Set a test cap and expected activated-user count before spending. Increase one channel's budget only after acceptable retention and acquisition cost are demonstrated.
- Target 900 cumulative registrations, 400 monthly meaningful active users, 110 weekly meaningful active users, 45% activation, and 18% four-week retention.
- Agree one commercial test: paid consumer feature or sponsored/partner activity, depending on observed demand. Write the offer, intended buyer, direct costs, and success criteria before implementation.
- Conduct an incident/support drill and review production access and recovery procedures.

**Exit decision:** A launch date does not override readiness. Weak conversion or churn triggers a narrower launch and product correction.

### Month 5 — Repeat what works and test payment

**Owners:** Growth, product, CEO.

- Repeat the strongest acquisition test with a new batch or period. Compare activated-user cost and retained users against the first test.
- Run one commercial pilot with a bounded product effort of no more than two weeks. A proposed minimum signal is one external paying partner or ten non-staff paying users, depending on the chosen model. This is an experiment threshold, not a revenue forecast.
- Track cash collected, delivery costs, refunds, repeated usage and stated reasons for buying. For a consumer offer, measure conversion among eligible users actually exposed to it.
- Target 1,800 cumulative registrations, 700 monthly meaningful active users, 200 weekly meaningful active users, 45% activation, and 20% four-week retention.
- Improve referral attribution and invite usefulness. Count actual recipient activation; a share-button tap is insufficient evidence.
- Add support capacity only if the workload and financial gates are met.

**Exit decision:** If commercial interest fails to convert, revise or reject the offer. If acquisition deteriorates materially when repeated, the channel is not yet repeatable.

### Month 6 — Decide whether the company is ready to expand

**Owners:** CEO with every function owner.

- Target 3,000 cumulative registrations, 1,000 monthly meaningful active users, 300 weekly meaningful active users, 50% activation, and at least 20% four-week retention.
- Document one channel that produces appropriately retained users in two separate tests, including direct and fully loaded costs.
- Complete the commercial pilot report and decide whether there is sufficient evidence to invest further in monetization. Revenue may still be zero if no suitable offer has been validated; do not manufacture a target detached from the business model.
- Review cash, team capacity, user evidence, reliability, commercial learning and acquisition economics. Prepare a next-six-month plan and an evidence-based company update.

**Decision:** Expand modestly when repeat use, distribution and financing support it; continue focused validation when evidence is promising but incomplete; narrow or change direction when repeated experiments fail. Revenue, downloads, or press alone are insufficient grounds to expand the team.

## 5. KPI scorecard and definitions

| Metric | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 | M6 | Owner |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Cumulative eligible registrations | Baseline | 150 | 400 | 900 | 1,800 | 3,000 | Growth |
| Monthly meaningful active users | Baseline | 75 | 200 | 400 | 700 | 1,000 | Product |
| Weekly meaningful active users | Baseline | 20 | 50 | 110 | 200 | 300 | Product |
| New-user activation | Measure | 35% | 40% | 45% | 45% | 50% | Product |
| Four-week retention of activated users | Not available | Early cohorts only | 15% | 18% | 20% | 20%+ | Product |
| Weekly users receiving a peer response | Define | Measure | 20% | 25% | 30% | 30%+ | Community |
| Crash-free sessions | Establish | 99%+ | 99.3%+ | 99.5%+ | 99.5%+ | 99.5%+ | Engineering |
| Initial human support response, staffed weekdays | Establish | Within 1 business day | Same | Same | Same | Same | Community |
| Cash forecast and dashboard review | Weekly | Weekly | Weekly | Weekly | Weekly | Weekly | CEO |

The active-user targets are separate goals, not arithmetic consequences of registrations. Revise the volume targets after Month 2 using observed cohort retention, channel capacity, and available cash. Retention thresholds are internal decision rules, not sourced industry standards.

**North-star candidate:** weekly users receiving a meaningful response from another real person on their ranking. This is appropriate only if Tacto is the social product described here. Define a response as a comment, remix, or comparison by a distinct non-staff account; exclude bots, self-actions, and sample posts. Start with a target of 90 such users per week at Month 6, alongside the 300 weekly active target. Watch response concentration so a small clique does not conceal an empty experience for others.

**Eligible registration:** a unique real account that completes signup; exclude duplicates, bots, staff/test accounts. Track campaign attribution separately.

**Activation:** eligible new registrations completing the agreed core value action within seven days divided by eligible registrations whose seven-day window has elapsed. For the assumed music product, start with publishing one ranking of at least five picks. Track sharing and peer response as subsequent funnel steps rather than making activation depend entirely on an initially sparse network.

**Meaningful activity:** publishing, commenting, remixing, or completing a comparison. Report weekly unique users over the last seven days and monthly unique users over the last 30 days. Mere app opens do not qualify.

**Four-week retention:** activated users who perform another meaningful action between Days 28 and 34 after activation, divided by activated users whose full Day 34 window has elapsed. Use the same timing convention throughout. Report numerator, denominator, audience, acquisition source, and incentive status. Do not compare this metric with exact Day 30 or rolling retention. Analytics retention definitions differ, so choose an explicit window and keep it consistent: [Amplitude retention documentation](https://amplitude.com/docs/analytics/charts/retention-analysis/retention-analysis-interpret).

**Acquisition cost:** campaign cash cost divided by attributable activated users, plus a separately reported fully loaded version including allocated labor, creative, and creator costs. Also calculate cost per acquired user retained at four weeks. Until payment and contribution margin are measured, any acceptable cost cap is an experiment budget constraint, not proof of profitable economics.

**Revenue and commercial economics:** distinguish booked commitments, cash collected, recognized revenue, recurring revenue, and contribution after direct costs. Do not call a one-off sponsored pilot monthly recurring revenue. Do not estimate lifetime value from immature cohorts.

## 6. Marketing and social media operating plan

### Positioning and audience

Select one audience first and test one simple promise. For the potential music app: “Make your music ranking. See how your friends would order it.” Test that language with users before adopting it as the final brand message.

Use Instagram plus one community or video surface where the chosen audience is already active. Choose the second surface after interviews and small tests. LinkedIn is optional for founder/company updates and partnerships; it should not become a daily production obligation without evidence.

### Sustainable weekly content cadence

- Two short videos: one participatory opinion/ranking prompt and one product demonstration or real community story.
- One carousel or static ranking designed for discussion.
- One user contribution, creator feature, or product-learning post, with appropriate permission.
- Stories or quick community updates on three to five days, mostly repurposed from the main production batch.
- One recurring community challenge in Months 2–3, increasing to two only when participation and moderation capacity justify it.
- One weekly roundup from Month 3 if an opted-in audience wants it.

Suggested content balance: 40% audience participation, 30% user/creator examples, 20% clear product utility, 10% company progress. Treat this as a starting mix; change it using conversion and participation results.

Example weekly production: Monday review the prior week's results and select prompts; Tuesday batch filming and design; Wednesday publish the main challenge; Thursday publish a useful demonstration and respond to contributors; Friday feature users and record learning. Reserve 30–45 minutes each staffed weekday for replies and support. Reduce output if this routine exceeds capacity.

### Social and campaign KPIs

Record by platform and format: reach, views, shares, saves, substantive replies, profile visits, outbound clicks, attributed registrations, activated users, and retained users. Do not mix reach and views in rate denominators or add audiences across platforms as though they were unique people.

Month 1 establishes baselines. From Month 2, aim to fulfill at least 85% of the agreed content schedule and find two formats that repeatedly attract the selected audience. By Month 3, select one format based on activated users per production hour, not a viral outlier. By Month 6, owned social, creator activity and community partnerships should collectively deliver enough registrations to support the planned monthly acquisition; if they cannot, reduce the forecast rather than inventing a follower target.

Use distinct campaign links and an optional “How did you hear about us?” question; mobile attribution will be incomplete. Report unattributed users honestly. Reusing permissioned footage across platforms is appropriate; creating a separate daily channel operation for every platform is not required.

Creator pilots: start with three to five relevant prospects, not a large ambassador program. Require a clear brief, disclosure where applicable, tracking, and a specific participation action. Record creator fees separately and distinguish paid contributors from voluntary repeat users.

### Month 6 acquisition feasibility check

The target requires about 1,200 new registrations in Month 6. One illustrative funnel is 6,000 qualified visits at 20% visit-to-registration conversion, producing 1,200 registrations; at 50% activation that yields 600 newly activated users. These conversion rates are unvalidated assumptions, and this funnel does not automatically produce the active-user targets. Model retained existing users separately.

If conversion is only 10%, the same registration target needs 12,000 qualified visits. If the small team cannot produce that traffic economically, plan a smaller audience with better repeat use. Do not make the plan depend on viral content.

## 7. Partnerships, sales and monetization

For a consumer app, maintain a small founder-led partnership/commercial function. Distinguish distribution partners from paying customers.

- Month 1: map 20 relevant communities, creators or potential commercial buyers; identify their incentives.
- Month 2: conduct five substantive conversations and secure two beta/distribution collaborators where appropriate.
- Month 3: execute two tracked distribution pilots and conduct the commercial interviews.
- Month 4: choose one paid offer based on evidence and confirm the decision-maker, budget, deliverable, and timeline.
- Month 5: collect actual payment in the bounded test if demand exists.
- Month 6: assess repeat purchase, willingness to renew, delivery effort and contribution. Continue only if the test supports a repeatable offer.

Maintain a tracker with owner, relationship type, stage, expected value, last contact, next action and date. Monthly review should distinguish conversations, agreed pilots, signed commitments and collected money. A contact list is not a pipeline of confirmed revenue.

Uncommitted fundraising is not available cash.

If resources are materially lower, use a founder-led plan: fewer channels, fixed-scope contractors, no generalist hire until affordable, smaller beta cohorts, and minimal paid testing. Rebuild targets from capacity; do not retain the same targets while removing the people needed to deliver them.

Review actual cash versus plan weekly, forecast the next 13 weeks, close management accounts monthly, and approve campaign caps before commitments. A planned expenditure ceiling is not a spending target.

## 9. Operations, risk and governance

Before wider social access, assign an owner for privacy/data practices, music metadata and artwork usage permissions, account deletion, moderation, and applicable store requirements. Obtain specialist review where required by the actual geography and audience. This document does not determine those obligations.

Keep company accounts under company-controlled access, maintain a record of who can access production, document backup/recovery and incident contacts, and retain contracts and work ownership records. Review access when anyone leaves.

| Risk | Early warning | Owner and response |
|---|---|---|
| Weak repeat value | Mature cohorts repeatedly miss retention thresholds | Product owner narrows the core task; growth pauses expansion |
| Empty social experience | Few new users receive real responses | Community owner seeds one focused group and tracks incentives |
| Prototype scope exceeds capacity | Core milestones slip for two weeks | Technical founder reduces scope; CEO resets launch and volume targets |
| Acquisition fails when repeated | Retained-user cost rises or conversion collapses | Growth owner reruns a bounded test or rejects the channel |
| Spend grows before demand | Hiring commitments increase while use stagnates | CEO freezes additional commitments and updates forecast |
| Harm, access or data failure | Reports, permission failures, or lost data | Named incident owner contains and investigates; expansion pauses |
| Founder/generalist overload | Support/content repeatedly displaces core work | CEO reduces cadence or purchases bounded help |

Reliability guardrails: critical account/data incidents require immediate triage; paid acquisition pauses if crash-free sessions fall below 99% for a sustained release period. Low-volume measurements need incident-level investigation rather than blind reliance on percentages.

## 10. Management rhythm

- **Monday, 30 minutes:** review company scorecard and cash; set three company priorities maximum, each with owner and due date.
- **Midweek, 45 minutes:** user evidence and product decision review. Choose the most consequential friction to resolve.
- **Friday, 45 minutes:** product demo, campaign outcomes, support themes, and decisions. Record what changed and why.
- **Month-end, 90 minutes:** review milestone evidence, mature cohorts, actual costs, capacity and risks. Decide explicitly whether to proceed, hold, reduce scope, or change direction.

Each owner posts a short weekly update: target, actual, explanation, next action, and decision needed. Keep one shared dashboard and one action tracker. Activity counts supplement outcomes; they do not replace them.

## 12. Evidence and outstanding decisions

The company approach prioritizes learning before increasing headcount and expenditure, consistent with [Y Combinator's discussion of premature scaling](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/the-real-product-market-fit/). Qualitative observation complements product metrics; see [YC's user observation guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/practical-design-user-observation/). These sources inform the operating approach, not this draft's numerical targets or costs.

To turn this into Tacto's committed plan, resolve five questions: What does Tacto sell or intend to sell? Who is the first customer/user and in what geography? What team and commitments already exist? What are current traction and revenue? How much unrestricted cash and monthly expenditure are available? Then revise the hiring, funnel, commercial and budget tables together.
